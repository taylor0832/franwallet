# The registration states: why where you buy changes the process

Franchising is regulated twice. Federally, the FTC's Franchise Rule applies everywhere — that's what makes the FDD exist at all. But roughly a quarter of states add their own layer: the franchisor must **register the franchise (or file) with the state before it can legally sell to you there**, and some states review the offering before approving it.

## The registration states

The states generally requiring **pre-sale registration and review** of the FDD:

**California, Hawaii, Illinois, Indiana, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota, New York, North Dakota, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Virginia, Washington, Wisconsin** — with Michigan and Wisconsin operating as simpler *notice-filing* states (they file, the state doesn't substantively review).

A further group (including Connecticut, Florida, Kentucky, Nebraska, Texas, Utah and others) has **business-opportunity laws** with franchise exemptions that usually require a one-time or annual filing.

*The exact list shifts with legislation — treat this as the map, and verify your state's current rule before relying on it.*

## Why you should care as a buyer

**1. It's a free background check.** In registration states, examiners have already read the FDD and pushed back on it. Some states (notably CA, MD, IL) publish the filings — you can look up your franchisor's registration status and sometimes prior versions of their FDD for free on the state's site. A franchisor that keeps failing to register cleanly in the strict states is telling you something.

**2. It affects *which* FDD you get.** Registration states often force state-specific addenda — and those addenda are frequently *more protective of you* than the base agreement (limits on termination, choice-of-law carve-outs, guarantee modifications). Read your state's addendum; it may quietly grant rights the main contract denies.

**3. It affects timing.** A franchisor whose registration has lapsed in your state legally cannot sell to you until it's renewed — registrations typically renew annually. If your closing is stalled on "we're waiting on the state," this is what's happening.

**4. It catches financial weakness.** Several registration states impose **financial assurance requirements** (escrow of your franchise fee, fee deferral until the unit opens, or a surety bond) on franchisors with weak balance sheets. If your franchisor must defer fees in Maryland, that's Item 21 telling you something in plainer language.

## The multi-state wrinkle for operators

Registration follows a mix of where *you* live, where the *unit* will operate, and where the offer was made. If you live in Texas but you're buying a unit in Illinois, Illinois registration matters. Multi-unit developers crossing state lines should confirm registration in every target state before signing an area development agreement that assumes them.

## The two-minute check

1. Find your state (and the unit's state) in the lists above.
2. If it's a registration state, search "[state] franchise registration lookup" and confirm your franchisor is **currently registered**.
3. Ask for the **state addendum** to the FDD and read it side-by-side with Item 17.
