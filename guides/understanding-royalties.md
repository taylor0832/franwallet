# Understanding royalties (and the ad fund)

The royalty is the price of the playbook: the brand, the systems, the supply chain, the customers who already know the name. Whether it's *worth* that price is the entire franchise decision. Here's how the mechanics actually work.

## Percentage vs. fixed

**Percentage royalties** (the overwhelming majority): typically **4–8% of gross sales**, paid weekly or monthly. Note the word *gross* — it's a cut of revenue, not profit. The franchisor gets paid whether your unit made money or not. A 6% royalty on a unit doing $900k is $54,000 a year, in good years and bad ones.

**Fixed royalties** (a flat weekly/monthly fee): more common in service franchises. Brutal at low volume, a bargain at high volume — a $500/week flat fee is 13% of a $200k unit's revenue and 2.6% of a $1M unit's.

**Minimums**: many percentage agreements hide a floor ("6% or $1,500/month, whichever is greater"). In a slow ramp, the minimum *is* your royalty. Check Item 6 of the FDD for it.

## The ad fund is a second royalty

On top of the royalty, most systems charge **1–4% of gross sales** into a national or regional marketing fund, and many *also* require minimum local marketing spend. Combined royalty + ad fund of **8–10% of gross** is normal in food concepts. Two things worth knowing:

- The fund pays for system-wide marketing — you're not guaranteed a dollar of it lands in your market. Item 11 of the FDD describes how it's spent; franchisee advisory councils exist because operators fight about exactly this.
- Reconcile it. Royalty and ad-fund draws are computed on *reported* sales, and reporting errors (POS misconfigurations, refund handling, catering channels) are common enough that checking the math monthly is worth 20 minutes.

## What royalties mean for your P&L

The royalty is why franchise unit economics are tighter than independent ones. An independent restaurant at 30% COGS, 28% labor, 10% occupancy keeps what a franchisee at the same numbers doesn't — the ~8 points that went to the system. That's not an argument against franchising; it's the reason your other lines must be run tighter than an independent's. You bought a proven model; the royalty is the mortgage on it.

The break-even question to run before you sign: at the median Item 19 revenue, subtract COGS, labor, occupancy, *and the full royalty + ad fund + minimum local spend* — is the remainder a business? Run it again at 80% of median revenue, because roughly half of units are below the median.

## Three contract details that bite later

1. **Royalty on gross, refunds included?** How are refunds, discounts, and third-party delivery fees treated in "gross sales"? The definition in your agreement decides.
2. **Technology fees**: many modern systems add $200–600/month in required software fees. It's a royalty with a different name; count it as one.
3. **Increases at renewal**: your 5% royalty is for *this* term. Item 17 tells you what renewal looks like — often "then-current terms," meaning the rate the franchisor charges new buyers in ten years.
