# What is an FDD? The plain-English breakdown

The Franchise Disclosure Document is the single most important document you'll read before buying a franchise — and most buyers skim it. It's long (often 200+ pages), it's written by the franchisor's lawyers, and it's legally required to tell you things the sales team never will.

Federal law requires the franchisor to give it to you **at least 14 days before you sign anything or pay anything**. Use those 14 days.

## The 23 items, grouped by what they actually tell you

**Who you're getting into business with (Items 1–4)**
The franchisor, its parents and affiliates, the executives running it, litigation history, and bankruptcies. A pattern of lawsuits from franchisees — not customers — is the flag that matters.

**What it costs (Items 5–7)**
The franchise fee, ongoing fees (royalty, ad fund, technology fees), and the full estimated initial investment. Item 7 is the number to stress-test: franchisors are allowed to give ranges, and real openings routinely land at the top of the range or above it.

**The rules you'll live under (Items 8–17)**
Required suppliers (Item 8 — where franchisors often earn rebates on your purchases), your obligations, territory rights (Item 12 — read whether your "territory" is actually exclusive), trademarks, and the renewal/termination/transfer terms in Item 17. Item 17 is where you learn how hard it is to leave.

**Whether the math works (Items 19–21)**
Item 19 is the Financial Performance Representation — the only place a franchisor can legally show you revenue or profit numbers. If there's no Item 19, ask why. Item 20 shows how many units opened, closed, and transferred over three years; a brand that's quietly churning units shows it here. Item 21 is the franchisor's own audited financials — a franchisor in weak financial shape is your risk, not theirs.

**The paperwork (Items 22–23)**
Every contract you'll sign, and the receipt that starts your 14-day clock.

## The three questions the FDD answers if you make it

1. **Item 7 vs. your capital**: can you fund the top of the range plus 6 months of operating losses?
2. **Item 19 vs. Item 6**: at the revenue they show, what's left after royalty, ad fund, labor, COGS, and rent?
3. **Item 20 + calls**: the FDD lists every current and former franchisee with phone numbers. Call five current, and two who left. Former franchisees are the highest-signal calls you will ever make.

## Don't do this alone

A franchise attorney review of an FDD typically runs $1,500–$3,500 and pays for itself the first time it catches a personal guarantee or a liquidated damages clause you didn't see.
