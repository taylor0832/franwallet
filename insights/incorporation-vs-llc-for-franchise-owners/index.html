What does incorporation mean?

Incorporation generally means creating a corporation under state law. Forming a limited liability company creates an LLC instead. Both involve company records, but the ownership, governance and tax questions should be evaluated for the actual business.

Franchise owners sometimes use incorporation as shorthand for any entity setup. Clarify which entity you mean before asking a provider to file.

Separate legal structure from tax classification

An LLC is a state-law structure. Its federal tax treatment depends on ownership and elections. An S corporation is a federal tax classification for an eligible entity, not simply another name for an LLC. Do not assume that filing a company also makes a tax election.

Bring these franchise-specific questions

  • Who will own the company, and who can sign for it?
  • Where will it operate and where does it need registration?
  • Which entity must appear on the franchise agreement and lease?
  • What does the lender require of the borrower and guarantors?
  • Will locations share a company or require separate entities?
  • What are the state fees, professional fees and ongoing administrative duties?

Compare the work, not a promised tax saving

Ask your attorney and accountant to compare governance, ownership, compensation, filing obligations and state treatment. There is no single profit threshold or structure that fits every franchise owner. A service provider should explain what it files and what remains your responsibility.

Turn the decision into an organized setup

Record the chosen structure, approved legal name, state and owners after the appropriate review. Retain state approval and governing documents; then complete applicable EIN, banking and registration steps.
