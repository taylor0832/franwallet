Keep three records distinct

The debt register says what you have recorded. The lender statement says what the lender reports for a date or period. Bank activity shows cash movement. Compare them; one is not an automatic substitute for the others.

An illustrative principal review

Assume a $50,000 opening principal balance, a $1,200 payment and a lender-confirmed $300 interest portion. With no fees, new advances or other adjustments, $900 reduces principal and the expected closing principal is $49,100. Compare that expectation with the lender's statement.

Do not subtract the entire $1,200 payment from principal. A payoff quote may differ from a statement balance because of accrued interest, fees or other terms.

Work through the monthly checklist

  1. Confirm the borrower, location and obligation.
  2. Check the statement date and the prior recorded balance.
  3. Find payment activity and any potential match.
  4. Use lender information to understand principal, interest and adjustments.
  5. Investigate unexplained differences with the lender or accountant.
  6. Update your record and its source date after review.

Leave a useful note when something does not match

Record the dates, amounts and source documents involved, along with the open question and responsible person. Do not force the figures to match by changing an unsupported balance. If an older statement is all you have, keep that date visible.

Make the next review easier

Keep statements beside the location's debt records in FranWallet. A possible bank-payment match is a review aid, not verification of principal. For accounting postings, interest treatment or complex loan terms, work with your accountant.
