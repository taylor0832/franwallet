Start with the capital need

Separate purchase or opening costs, working capital, reserves and ongoing payments. Identify which figures are supported by documents and which are assumptions. A lender or financing professional can explain available structures and the requirements for your situation.

SBA-backed borrowing

The SBA 7(a) program guarantees qualifying lender loans; borrowers work with lenders. Requirements, documentation, terms and timing depend on the transaction and lender. Use current official guidance and an actual lender request rather than assuming a universal credit score, down payment or approval timeline.

Conventional borrowing

A conventional loan follows the lender's underwriting and commercial terms. Ask about collateral, guarantees, rate changes, payment schedules and fees. Compare the full proposal, including ongoing obligations, with your operating assumptions.

ROBS and retirement assets

Rollovers as Business Start-ups involve retirement-plan and corporate arrangements. Using retirement assets introduces investment and ongoing compliance questions. Obtain specialized tax, legal and plan advice rather than treating the structure as a simple withdrawal or a universally preferable source of capital.

Prepare for the discussion

  • What amount and use of funds are being evaluated?
  • What documents and owner contribution does the lender require?
  • What guarantees or collateral are involved?
  • How would a different sales or cost scenario affect cash payments?
  • Who handles ongoing plan, reporting or loan requirements?
