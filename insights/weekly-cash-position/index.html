What is a weekly cash position?

A weekly cash position combines a dated starting balance with expected cash receipts and payments. Its purpose is to make timing visible. It is different from a profit-and-loss statement and is only as reliable as the balances, obligations and assumptions you include.

Use the same dates and definitions

  • List the accounts and locations included and the date of the opening balances.
  • Record expected cash receipts in the week they are likely to settle.
  • Include payroll, rent, royalties, advertising funds, taxes, vendor and debt payments.
  • Identify restricted or already committed cash separately.
  • Set your own planning buffer and record the assumptions behind it.

A simple worked example

Illustrative week: opening cash of $40,000 plus $20,000 expected receipts minus $35,000 expected payments leaves $25,000 projected closing cash. Against a chosen $10,000 buffer, planning headroom is $15,000. This is not verified cash available to distribute or transfer.

Weekly totals can hide a shortfall before a deposit arrives. Review the dates of the largest receipts and payments as well as the total.

Keep locations and transfers clear

Prepare each location's position first. Record a transfer as a separate movement in both affected accounts so it is not counted as operating income or expense. A portfolio total does not mean cash can legally or practically move between companies. Confirm restrictions and any proposed movement with the responsible people.

Update the worksheet each week

Replace forecasts with actual results, explain the difference and refresh the next week's dates. A rolling forecast can extend the horizon, but missing receipts or obligations should remain visible.
